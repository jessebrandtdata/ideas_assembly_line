This page measures the introduced stage, the one stage every Congress has: CRS writes summaries for introduced versions only in the 119th Congress ("CRS is not writing bill summaries for subsequent versions of measures as they move through the legislative process", Congress.gov help, Bill Summaries), while earlier Congresses' summaries also cover later versions. Each lag runs from the date printed on the introduced text to the bill's first CRS summary, whatever version that summary describes. For – bills in view the first summary describes a later version (a reported bill or the public law, say) because CRS never summarized the introduced one; their lag runs to that summary. The – summaries of later versions in view are kept in the data under their stage. Every lag starts at the date printed on the bill, the one start date every Congress has; the text usually becomes readable online days to weeks later, so lags read that much longer than the wait a reader sees. Proposal IV.2 counts pages of bill text; the source data has no page count yet, so the page sizes bills in characters or sections of their parsed text instead. The summary date is the earliest the summary record retains. Summaries first captured before the spicy-regs warehouse began keeping revisions (2026-11-06) carry only their latest revision, so a summary revised later reads slower than it was. – introduced summaries in view are dated after their Congress ended (on January 2 of the year after its second session): very late summaries, or re-publications of earlier ones.
